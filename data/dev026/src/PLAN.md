## What the application will do

The normal workflow is: choose instruments and dates → check data quality → choose an explicit strategy and costs → inspect example signals → run historical tests → compare with simple alternatives and random controls → freeze selected rules → test on previously withheld dates → inspect trades and export a report.

The interface will explain terms in plain English. For example, a short position seeks to profit from a price decline; a stop is a simulated exit intended to limit a loss; a holdout is historical data withheld during strategy development. No statistical badge will be labeled as proof of future profitability.

## Initial engineering choices

These are proposed defaults, chosen to reduce hidden assumptions. They are not findings from backtests.

- Start verification on SPY, QQQ, IWM and DIA, followed by an explicit stock watchlist such as AAPL and MSFT. These are test fixtures, not investment recommendations or a representative historical stock universe.
- Validate hourly and daily signal generation first. Both use underlying minute data to model execution when available. Then enable 1-, 5-, 15-minute and 4-hour strategies after the same execution checks pass. All ten setup families remain in the planned scope.
- Start with regular exchange hours, with actual holidays, early closes and daylight-saving changes. Extended hours will be a distinct configuration with its own data and cost assumptions. NYSE identifies the core session as 09:30–16:00 ET. [NYSE calendar](https://www.nyse.com/markets/hours-calendars)
- Make hourly bars session-anchored, preserving the final shorter bar. For a normal equity session, 4-hour aggregation produces a 4-hour bar and a 2.5-hour remainder. Identify those bars explicitly; do not silently splice trading days or call both full 4-hour bars.
- Use only information available at the decision time. A signal based on a completed candle normally fills at the next eligible minute's open, including the next trading session for a daily close. Offer the video's same-close assumption only as a clearly labeled alternate model.
- Use actual tradable prices for simulated fills and a tested split/corporate-action policy for indicators, share quantities and cash flows. Include dividends in positions and benchmark returns where relevant; short positions owe distributions. Preserve raw and adjusted representations and the adjustment metadata.
- Default to whole shares, a defined initial capital, explicit exposure limits, and configurable fixed-fraction risk sizing. Proposed demo settings: $100,000 starting capital, at most 0.5% planned risk per trade, 25% gross exposure per symbol and 100% gross exposure overall. These are simulation settings, not advice. Gaps can exceed the planned loss.
- Show per-strategy, per-symbol results separately from a shared-capital portfolio. A portfolio must apply cash, simultaneous-position, sizing and exposure constraints; summing independent strategy profits is not a portfolio backtest.
- Model commission, regulatory/broker fees, spread, slippage and gap fills separately. Scenario values are assumptions unless supported by a versioned source. Run baseline and higher-cost scenarios.
- Support long and short simulations. Show borrowing-cost sensitivity, for example 0%, 3%, 10% and 30% annualized as illustrative scenarios. Historical borrow availability, recalls, collateral and broker-specific margin are not established by ordinary price bars. Label short results as conditional on the chosen availability and financing model; unavailable borrow rejects the trade. No short result is described as executable solely because the simulated profit is positive.

## Validation design

Separate three questions: did the software implement its rules, did the historical pattern beat the specified alternatives, and are the assumptions plausible enough to use the result? A good answer to one does not answer the others.

1. **Engine correctness:** synthetic, hand-calculated paths for long and short trades; gaps; limit/stop ordering; fees and borrowing across non-trading days; distributions; simultaneous signals; cash constraints; session changes and splits. Compare an intentionally simple reference engine against the optimized engine on small datasets. Use an independent set of accounting identities to reconcile balances.
2. **No future information:** append/truncate tests for every detector and signal, confirmed-pivot availability timestamps, closed higher-timeframe candles only, and next-eligible-bar fills. Verify that data after a cutoff cannot change decisions before it. Keep a deliberately future-peeking test as an isolated negative-control fixture, unavailable as a runnable user strategy.
3. **Honest measurement:** mark positions to market each day; calculate returns on defined capital; report net profit, return, exposure, worst drawdown, Sharpe definition, trade count, win rate, profit/loss ratio, and uncertainty. The video's realized-exit-day P&L convention may be shown only as a separate compatibility statistic.
4. **Comparisons:** cash and buy-and-hold benchmarks on the same dates with distributions and comparable capital/exposure; matched random entries and random levels with fixed seeds; time/volatility-aware matching; paired ablations that change one rule at a time.
5. **Research discipline:** freeze discovery dates, definitions, thresholds, candidate-selection logic and trial registry before results. Add time-ordered validation and a later final holdout. Record amendments and all holdout reads. Once viewed, that holdout is labeled consumed; a revised strategy needs fresh evidence rather than relabeling the same dates unseen.
6. **Search uncertainty:** report total configurations and distinct experiments, family/timeframe dependence and failed/insufficient samples. Use session/block bootstrap intervals, and an explicitly specified search-aware correction such as Deflated Sharpe Ratio plus sensitivity to the effective trial count. This is a proposed improvement: searching many configurations inflates the best observed Sharpe. [Bailey and López de Prado paper](https://davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf)
7. **Source limitations:** ordinary historical testing does not prove a strategy works in live trading. SPY and QQQ also share market exposure, so agreement between them is a replication check rather than independent evidence of universal performance. Do not copy futures profits, returns, win rates or pass counts into equity results.

8. **Concentration:** report contributions of the best days and largest trades, including an explicitly descriptive version excluding the ten best days. Show both original and stressed results. This follows an on-screen source robustness claim and must not become an after-the-fact tradable exclusion rule.

The dated source example is NQ, four-hour, July 11, 2024. Preserve its screenshot as a future futures regression candidate; it cannot be validated with equity data or treated as a holdout-period example. Exact prices and adjustment conventions remain unavailable.

The video's 30-trade and Sharpe-0.5 gates can be available as a reference preset. They are not automatic reliability certification. Low sample sizes, wide confidence intervals, data faults, implausible costs or excessive selection can prevent a favorable conclusion even when profit is positive.
